Submarine telecommunication cables cross every ocean basin, and there is growing evidence that they can be turned into a dense, permanent observing infrastructure for the deep sea, a part of the ocean that remains largely unmonitored. Turning that potential into an operational reality requires far more than sensing technology: it raises questions of deployment and maintenance, environmental and societal impacts, data sharing, law, and maritime security.

This is the objective of the new [European Marine Board](https://www.marineboard.eu/) (EMB) Working Group on **["Submarine Cables: A Revolution in Ocean Monitoring"](https://www.marineboard.eu/submarine-cables)**, whose [kick-off meeting](https://www.marineboard.eu/kick-meeting-new-emb-working-group-submarine-cables) was held on **8 September 2026** at [IMREDD](https://imredd.univ-cotedazur.fr/), Université Côte d'Azur, in Nice, following an introductory meeting on 29 June 2026.

## Aims of the Working Group

The Working Group will produce a comprehensive state-of-the-art overview of the use of submarine cables for ocean monitoring, considering now obsolete, currently active, and newly laid telecommunication cables. Its scope covers:

- the monitoring opportunities offered by the different sensing technologies that can be applied to submarine cables, and their respective advantages and limitations;
- the practical requirements of deploying and maintaining such systems;
- their environmental and social implications;
- the legal implications of acquiring, sharing and using the resulting data;
- the maritime defence and security dimension, both for protecting the infrastructure and for detecting threats;
- research and policy recommendations to further develop the technology and its use in ocean observing.

A central question throughout is how cable-based systems can **complement the existing ocean observing system**, contributing to the Global Ocean Observing System and Essential Ocean Variables, and deliver a concrete service to society, from geohazard early warning to climate and biodiversity monitoring.

The group brings together **12 experts from across Europe**, combining expertise in submarine cable technology, cable-based sensing and the information it delivers, legal and geopolitical aspects, and the impacts of using such systems. I am chairing this working group with Luciana das Neves (CIIMAR, Portugal) as Co-Chair, and provides input to the [UN Decade of Ocean Science for Sustainable Development](https://oceandecade.org/).


The Working Group will deliver an **EMB Position Paper in late 2027**.
